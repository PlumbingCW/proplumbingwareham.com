The meter is running and nothing is on

Most calls about hidden leaks start with a symptom, not the word leak. A water bill that jumped, a meter dial that creeps with every fixture off, a warm spot on a floor, a stain that grew on a ceiling, or a hiss behind a wall at 2 a.m. all point the same way.

Those clues matter because they narrow the search before any tool comes out. A meter that moves with the house shut down means water is escaping somewhere between the meter and a fixture, and our job is to find where.

When a hidden leak is an emergency

A slow drip can wait for a morning appointment. The situations below cannot, so call the 24/7 line and use the list while you wait for a plumber.

  • ✓Water is dripping from a ceiling light, a fan or an outlet cover. Stay away, and shut off power to that circuit at the panel only if you can reach the panel without standing in water.
  • ✓The meter keeps spinning after you close every fixture and the floor or a wall is warm, soft or bubbling.
  • ✓You hear running water inside a wall or under the floor, or water pressure suddenly dropped.
  • ✓A basement or crawlspace is collecting water and you cannot see where it enters.
  • ✓Close the main shutoff valve, open the lowest faucet to drain the lines and take photos of every wet surface for your insurer.

If the main valve will not close, or you are not sure where it is, say so on the call. Dispatch can talk you through finding it, and a plumber can replace a failed valve once on site.

How we find a leak without tearing the wall open

Leak detection is a sequence, and each step rules out part of the house. We use several methods because no single tool works on every pipe or every building.

  1. 1

    Water meter test

    With every fixture closed, we read the meter and check the low-flow indicator, the small dial or triangle that turns with even a trickle. Movement means a leak exists. No movement points back to a fixture, a toilet flapper or a faulty reading.

  2. 2

    Pressure test

    We isolate the supply piping, apply a gauge and watch for a pressure drop over time. A steady needle clears that run of pipe. A falling needle tells us to split the system into smaller sections until the leaking run is isolated.

  3. 3

    Acoustic listening

    A ground microphone or a contact sensor picks up the hiss of pressurized water escaping a pipe. We sweep floors, walls and the yard, and the sound is loudest directly above the leak.

  4. 4

    Thermal imaging

    An infrared camera shows temperature differences behind drywall or across a floor. It is useful for hot-water lines, radiant heat and wet insulation, and we confirm what it shows with a moisture meter.

  5. 5

    Targeted opening

    Once the spot is known within a few inches, the plumber cuts one small access hole, repairs the pipe and tells you what the wall needs afterward.

Toilets, outdoor spigots and other quiet leaks

Before anyone digs, we rule out the cheap and common causes. A toilet flapper that leaks into the bowl can run for weeks, and a few drops of food coloring in the tank will show it. An outdoor spigot that was left connected to a hose can split inside the wall once the temperature drops.

Irrigation lines, a leaking water heater connection and a failed washer hose are also on the checklist. If the leak turns out to be at the tank, water heater repair is the next call, and a dripping fixture can be handled through faucet repair in the same visit.

Cottages that sat closed over winter

Wareham has a large seasonal housing stock, and a cottage that was shut off in the fall can wake up with a split pipe the first time the water is turned on in spring. The break often hides inside a wall or under the floor, and the meter will show it as soon as someone opens the main.

If you are reopening a seasonal home, turn the main on slowly, watch the meter with every fixture closed, and walk the house for wet spots before you leave. Anything you find on that first check is easier to fix than a leak that runs unattended for a week.

Leaks under the yard and outside the foundation

A buried line leaks without any sign inside the house, other than a bill or soft ground. Acoustic gear works well on pressurized pipe, and a plumber can also run a pressure test from the meter to the house to confirm the section that is losing water. If the leak sits between the meter and the foundation, water line repair is the fix.

In Wareham the water comes from either the Wareham Fire District or the Onset Fire District, and your plumber may need to coordinate with the district about the meter or shutoff. A leak on the street side of the meter is usually the district's concern, while the house side belongs to you. Ask the district who owns which part before you pay for excavation.

When we stop and say it is not a leak

Sometimes the answer is that nothing is leaking. A meter can look like it is moving because a softener is recharging, a toilet is refilling, or an ice maker cycled during the test. We repeat the meter test with those items accounted for and tell you plainly when the pipes are tight.

If the cause turns out to be a failed pipe, burst pipe repair covers the fix. Our dispatch also routes calls to Carver and Marion, so a plumber with detection gear may already be close by.
