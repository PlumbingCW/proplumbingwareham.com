The call Carver homes make most: no water after the lights go out

Carver has no town sewer, and the town's own hazard plan says most homes run on private wells. A well pump needs electricity, so every outage turns into a plumbing question: why is there no pressure, and is it the power or the system?

Sandy took power from nearly half the town in 2012, and the March 2018 storms left outages that ran five days and then another four. Emergency dispatch is available around the clock for exactly those stretches, including nights, weekends and holidays.

If the lights are on and the taps are still dry, call. The cause is often a tripped breaker, a failed pressure switch, a waterlogged tank or a pump that has quit, and a plumber can tell you which by phone before anyone drives out.

Problems a well-and-septic house produces

Not every house in Carver is on a well, but most are, and the same few faults show up again and again. Here is how we read them over the phone.

Pressure falls, then recovers

A pressure tank that has lost its air charge makes the pump start and stop every few seconds. That short cycling wears out the pump, so do not wait it out for weeks.

Water at the tap turns to sputter

Air in the line, a failing check valve or a pump drawing from a low water level. Dry spells matter here, since some Carver wells went dry in the 2016 drought.

Slow drains and gurgling toilets

With no sewer, the line runs to a tank and a leach field. Gurgling in several fixtures points at the tank outlet, the distribution box or the pipe between them.

Wet floor near the water heater

Tank corrosion, a weeping relief valve or a loose connection. Water heater repair fixes many of these, and a rusted-through tank means replacement.

Damp basement after heavy rain

Groundwater is high in much of town. A failing sump pump leaves a cellar exposed, and sump pump service is a same-day call when the pit is filling.

Before we arrive: if you have a burst pipe or active leak

A few minutes of work from you limits the damage while the plumber is on the way. On a private well the steps differ slightly from a town-water house.

Find the main shutoff

On town or district water, the main valve is where the line enters the house. On a well, it is usually near the pressure tank. Turn it fully closed, then open a low faucet to drain the pipes.

Stop the pump

If you are on a well, switch off the pump breaker so the pressure tank stops refilling a broken line. Reach the panel only if the floor around it is dry.

Keep people out of wet areas

Water around outlets, a furnace or a water heater is a shock risk. Keep children and pets clear and do not wade into a flooded cellar.

Call the 24/7 line

Give the dispatcher your address, the fixture or pipe that failed and whether the water is stopped. Move valuables up off the floor while you wait.

Iron, sediment and the water heater

Carver's public systems treat for iron and manganese. The Town Hall well adds hexametaphosphate, and North Carver Water District uses permanganate and filtration. Private wells in the same ground can carry the same minerals.

That sediment settles in the bottom of a tank-type heater and in aerators and valves, so a rumbling heater or a rusty first draw is worth a call. When the tank itself is failing, water heater replacement is usually safer than a repair, and a plumber can size the new unit on the first visit.

Manufactured homes and winter freezes

Roughly a quarter of Carver's housing is manufactured or mobile homes, many in 55-plus communities such as South Meadow Village and Cranberry Village. Supply lines in the underbelly can freeze in a hard cold snap, and a split pipe there may not show until the thaw.

If you smell wet insulation or see water dripping from the skirt, shut the main and call the 24/7 line. Slow leaks under a home are a case for leak detection before anyone cuts into a floor.

Permits in Carver

Carver's Building Department says water heaters, backflow preventers, new fixtures and plumbing remodels need a plumbing or gas permit, and a licensed plumber must call in the inspection. The plumbing and gas inspector is in on set days, starting at 8 a.m., so ask when the next slot is.

Emergency repairs of a leak do not usually need a permit, but replacing a heater does. When in doubt, ask the town's Building Department before work starts.

Which jobs need a permit in Carver

Plumbing permits in Carver run through the town's Building Department, and the state rule behind them is 248 CMR 3.05. It exempts clearing a stoppage and fixing a leak inside a faucet or valve. A pressure tank, a water heater or a replaced pipe run needs a permit pulled by a licensed plumber.
