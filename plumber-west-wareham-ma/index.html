Plumber in West Wareham, MA

West Wareham, ZIP code 02576, is one of the town's original village centers. It grew around a nineteenth-century iron works, and its surroundings are wooded, with houses set among trees.

A plumber's first job on an older house here is finding out what the house is connected to. This page explains how to settle that, what an old date on a building can and cannot tell you, and what a first visit covers.

A mill village with early houses

The Massachusetts Historical Commission's 1981 survey describes West Wareham as a well-preserved early nineteenth-century mill village. It names the Washington Iron Works of 1822, a stone-faced dam and foundations in a park-like setting, Federal-period cottages and a Greek Revival double house built for mill workers.

The 2018 master plan lists West Wareham with Wareham Village and Onset as a compact center, and it describes much of the rest of town as rural, with houses tucked behind trees.

A building date tells you when a house was built, not what is inside it. Pipes get replaced, kitchens get added and bathrooms move. The only way to know is to look, which is why a first visit starts with questions.

Four questions to settle on an older house here

Answer these and any plumber can quote more accurately, on the phone and on site.

Where does your water come from?

Wareham has two public districts, and some properties use private wells. Your water bill answers this in a minute. No bill usually means a well.

Where does the waste go?

Town sewer connects through a lateral to the main. Septic means a tank and a leach field. The Sewer Department at 508-295-6144 can confirm whether a street is sewered.

Where are the shutoffs?

Find the main valve and the valve at the water heater before you need them, and label both.

What was changed, and when?

Past permits at Inspectional Services show earlier plumbing work. Ask the seller, a neighbor or the town.

Town sewer or septic: what changes when you call

The 2022 housing plan says the town's wastewater upgrade did not extend sewer to the large undeveloped areas north of Route 25, and that areas with high septic failure rates are prioritized for connection. Whether your street is sewered is an address question, so do not assume.

If you are on town sewer

  • A backup points to the lateral or the main, so a camera look finds where the blockage sits.
  • The town owns the main, and where your side ends is a question for the Sewer Department.
  • Roots and old joints are common causes of repeat clogs in any buried line.

If you have septic

  • A backup may come from the tank outlet, the distribution box or the leach field.
  • Do not pump the tank to relieve a backup until the blockage is found.
  • A Title 5 inspection is part of selling the property.

Not sure what your house is connected to?

Permits and licenses in Wareham

A permit is required under 248 CMR 3.05 before plumbing work is installed, altered, replaced or repaired. Clearing a stoppage and fixing a leak inside a faucet or valve are exempt. The permit is issued to a licensed individual through Inspectional Services.

Look up any plumber in the state's Check a License tool, and ask for the license number first.

Source type: 248 CMR 3.00 and Wareham Inspectional Services
