When a sewer line failure is an emergency

A sewer line problem is urgent when waste has nowhere to go. Use this list to decide, and call the 24/7 line if any item fits.

  • ✓Sewage or gray water is coming up through a basement floor drain, a tub or a shower, and it gets worse each time anyone flushes.
  • ✓Every fixture in the house drains slowly or gurgles, not just one sink. That points past the branch lines to the main run outside.
  • ✓A sinkhole, soft patch or very green wet strip has appeared in the yard along the path of the pipe.
  • ✓A sewage smell is strong in the basement or crawlspace, or you can see standing wastewater near the foundation.
  • ✓A drain cleaner has already been through and the line backed up again within days. A cable that clears a line only briefly often means the pipe is damaged.

Until we arrive, do not run the washer, dishwasher or showers. If sewage is touching a furnace, a water heater or an outlet, stay clear of it and shut off power at the panel only when the floor around the panel is dry.

What a sewer lateral is and why it fails

Your sewer lateral is the single pipe that carries everything from the house to the town main, or to a septic tank on private ground. It usually leaves the building through the foundation wall or slab, runs a few feet underground and ends at a connection you cannot see. Ordinary drain cleaning deals with the fixtures and branch lines inside the house. This page covers the buried part.

Wareham's own housing plan puts the median home at about 1969 construction, with roughly one house in five built before 1939. That mix means a lot of laterals are clay, cast iron or fiber pipe that has been in the ground for decades, with joints that open slightly over time.

How buried sewer pipe breaks down

The failure mode decides the repair. These are the four we see on camera most often.

Root intrusion

Fine roots find a loose joint, grow inside the pipe and form a mat that catches paper. Cutting the roots helps for a season, and the joint still has to be sealed.

Bellies and sags

Soil under the pipe settles, a low spot forms and waste sits there. A belly causes repeat clogs even when the pipe is not cracked.

Cracks and offsets

A joint pulls apart or a section shifts sideways. Groundwater and soil can then enter the pipe, and sewage can leak out.

Collapse

Old fiber or clay pipe can flatten or crush, which stops flow completely. A collapse usually means excavation or pipe bursting.

How we find the problem before anything is dug

We start with the camera, not the shovel. Pulling the wrong section of lawn is the most expensive mistake in this trade.

Dig and replace, or line the pipe

Both methods work when matched to the right pipe. The camera findings, not a sales preference, should pick between them.

Excavated spot repair or replacement

  • Best for a short, clearly broken section such as one offset joint or a crushed run.
  • Lets us fix a belly by re-grading the pipe bed so it drains properly.
  • Disturbs lawn, walks or a driveway, and the surface has to be restored afterward.
  • Uses new PVC pipe and fittings with sound joints.

Trenchless lining or pipe bursting

  • Pipe lining pushes a resin-soaked liner through the old pipe and cures it into a new pipe inside the old one.
  • Pipe bursting pulls new pipe through the old path while breaking the old pipe outward.
  • Less digging, usually only an access pit at each end.
  • Not suited to a pipe with a deep belly, a full collapse or severe offsets. We will tell you plainly if it will not work.

Town sewer, septic tank or both

Wareham's Sewer Department runs about 61 miles of gravity pipe and 45 pump stations, and the town housing plan says the sewer could not be expanded north of Route 25. So one Wareham home may connect to a town main, while a neighbor a few miles away sends everything to a septic tank.

If your lateral runs to a town main, the question of who is responsible for which section is a question for the town, so ask the Sewer Department before assuming. If it runs to a septic tank, a failing pipe can also point to the tank inlet or a problem downstream. Title 5 inspections at a sale are done only by MassDEP-approved inspectors, and a plumber's camera findings can help explain a problem, but they do not replace that inspection.

What drives the price of a sewer line repair

Every sewer job is different, which is why we give you the number only after the camera has shown the pipe. These are the real drivers.

Length and depthA short repair a few feet down is quick. A long run, or one deeper than usual, needs more labor and shoring.
What sits on topLawn is easy to restore. A driveway, patio, tree root zone or foundation slab adds work.
MethodLining and bursting use specialized equipment and often mean less restoration work. Spot excavation is simpler equipment but more surface work.
Pipe material and connectionJoining to cast iron, clay or fiber pipe takes the correct couplings and sometimes extra pipe.
Permits and inspectionReplacing or repairing a sewer pipe is plumbing work, so a permit generally applies. We handle the paperwork through a licensed plumber, and the town's Inspectional Services office handles the inspection.

After-hours scheduling and weekends may change the schedule, but the clear price comes first. Ask your insurer too, because many homeowners policies treat sewer backup and buried line failure as optional coverage, and some exclude damage from long-term wear.

When this is not a do-it-yourself job

Sewage carries bacteria, so entering a flooded basement, handling wet materials or digging in the yard around a broken line is a health risk. Rental drain machines also do real damage to a cracked pipe, because a cutting head can snag at an offset and make a small break into a large one.

Digging near a sewer lateral also risks hitting gas, electric or water lines that run close by. A locator and a licensed plumber with a permit handle that safely. Also see sewer camera inspection if you only want to know the pipe condition before you buy a house or plan a renovation.
