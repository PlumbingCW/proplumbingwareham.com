Plumber in Swifts Beach, Wareham, MA

Swifts Beach is a beachside neighborhood in Wareham, with a town park at the end of Swifts Beach Road. The town's 2018 master plan lists it among the older medium-density neighborhoods and says they are experiencing some reinvestment.

That makes this page about renovation as much as repair. A house that is being updated needs the plumbing to be right, permitted and inspected. This page covers how to plan that and what questions to ask.

What the town says about this neighborhood

The 2018 master plan names Swifts Beach, Cranberry Grove and Shangri-La as examples of medium-density neighborhoods that are among the town's older ones and are seeing some reinvestment. That is the master plan's description, not a count of renovations.

A 2013 report in The Wareham Week says Swifts Beach was sewered in 1978 and quotes the sewer director saying much of the Swifts Beach system sits in groundwater, with a break repaired about fifteen feet underground. That is town infrastructure and years old, so ask the Sewer Department for current details at 508-295-6144.

Before you hire for a renovation

A plumbing plan that is clear up front saves rework. These questions are worth answering before work starts.

  • 1Ask for the plumber's license number and check it in the state's Check a License tool.
  • 2Ask who will pull the permit, and expect it to be issued to a licensed individual.
  • 3Ask for the scope in writing, including which fixtures, valves and lines are being replaced.
  • 4Ask whether the work needs the water off, and for how long.
  • 5Ask what the inspector will check, and when the inspection will be scheduled.
  • 6Keep a copy of the permit and the final sign-off with your house records.

Sewer, groundwater and your own lateral

The town owns the sewer main. The pipe between your house and the main is a separate matter, and where responsibility changes depends on the address, so ask the Sewer Department before assuming.

A sewer camera inspection shows the condition of your lateral and is a useful step before a major renovation, because it tells you whether the pipe will carry the new load. Our sewer inspection page explains what the camera finds.

What to expect on a renovation visit

A good plan is short and specific.

  1. 1

    Walk the house

    The plumber looks at the existing layout, fixtures and shutoffs.

  2. 2

    Check the connections

    Water, drain and, if relevant, the sewer lateral.

  3. 3

    Write the scope

    What will be replaced, what will stay and what it costs.

  4. 4

    Permit and schedule

    The permit is pulled, work is scheduled and the inspection is booked.

About Swifts Beach Park

In February 2025 the town administrator proposed using $200,000 from the Community Preservation Fund for Swifts Beach upgrades, including a porous walkway and benches, with no new parking. The final decision belonged to Town Meeting, so check with the town for what was approved.
