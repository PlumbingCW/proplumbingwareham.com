Coastal Bourne never really has an off season for pipes

Bourne sits at the head of Buzzards Bay, and Hurricane Bob drove a surge of 12 to 15 feet into the bay's northern shore in 1991, with Bourne among the places named by the National Weather Service. A basement here can see water from the ground, the sky or the bay, which is why emergency dispatch available around the clock is more than a slogan on this coast.

The town's housing plan counts 11,140 homes, and 2,368 of them, about one in five, are seasonal. Summer cottages that were built for July and August and later converted to year-round use often still have thin exposed lines in crawlspaces and porches. Those are the pipes that split when a cold snap hits an empty house.

Before we arrive: if you have a burst pipe or active leak in Bourne

Whether your house is on Bourne Water District water or a private well changes the first step. Here is the sequence for either, written for a Bourne house on a cold night or a flooded weekend.

Shut the main valve

On district water, the main is where the line enters the house, usually near the meter. On a private well, shut the valve after the pressure tank and turn off the well pump at its breaker so the tank stops refilling the leak.

Kill power near wet areas

If water is near the panel, outlets or a basement sump, cut those breakers only from a dry spot. If the floor around the panel is wet, do not touch it and tell the dispatcher.

Clear the area

Lift rugs and stored boxes, and shut off the water heater if the tank is empty or leaking. Keep children away from standing water and from any exposed wiring.

Call the 24/7 line

Tell the dispatcher your village, say if you are on town water or a well, and say if the house is currently lived in. A shut, empty cottage needs a plan for entry.

Pipe split in a shut-up cottage?

Shut off the main if you can reach it, then call the 24/7 line and we will book the next open licensed plumber.

Across the canal: bridges, traffic and planning the trip

The 8.1-mile Cape Cod Canal cuts through the northern part of town, and the Bourne Bridge and Sagamore Bridge, both built in 1933 to 1935, carry the traffic. Because the bridges are the only road crossings of the canal that most callers use, we ask which side you are on and which approach you would take.

Closing a Bourne house for the winter

A fall drain-down prevents most of the spring burst-pipe calls. These steps are what a licensed plumber would do, and some of them are best left to one.

  1. 1

    Shut the main and open the lowest faucet

    Turn off the incoming water and open every faucet so the lines can drain. Do the lowest fixture in the house first.

  2. 2

    Drain the water heater

    Turn off the gas or breaker, then drain the tank through the bottom valve to a floor drain or the yard.

  3. 3

    Blow out the lines

    Compressed air pushes remaining water out of low spots and traps in the line. This is a plumber's job on older houses where pipe sags hold water.

  4. 4

    Protect the traps and toilets

    Pour non-toxic plumbing antifreeze into traps and toilet bowls. Never use automotive antifreeze.

  5. 5

    Shut off the outside spigots

    Disconnect hoses and drain the sillcock, which is the exterior faucet that most often splits first.

Bourne water, wells and septic

Public water in Bourne comes from the Bourne Water District, which pumps groundwater from gravel-packed wells in the Monument Beach Town Forest, in Cataumet and on Joint Base Cape Cod. The district adds lime to raise the pH and limit corrosion, and in August 2023 it issued a boil-water order after a positive E. coli sample. After a notice like that, run the cold taps and flush the water heater, because stagnant lines hold the worst of it.

Not every Bourne home is on district water, and the Board of Health regulates private wells. Sewer reaches only part of town, including the Buzzards Bay village network and its newer treatment plant built in 2021, so many homes elsewhere are on septic. The town's housing plan warns that homes built before 1980, which make up about 57 percent of the stock, may have failing septic, so repeat backups call for a camera inspection before a repair is guessed at.

Signs a Bourne water heater is about to fail

Salt air and sandy sediment shorten the life of a tank. Check these before a cold shower becomes a flooded closet.

  • ✓Rust-colored hot water or a metallic taste only on the hot side.
  • ✓A rumbling or popping noise from the tank as sediment bakes on the bottom.
  • ✓Moisture or rust streaks at the base of the tank or around the flue.
  • ✓A tank that is more than 10 to 12 years old and has a corroded relief valve.
  • ✓A pilot light that will not stay lit on a gas heater in a house with a draft.

If any of these appear in the middle of the night, shut the cold supply to the tank and call. Water heater repair often saves a unit, and when it does not, replacement can be scheduled the same day when a slot is open.

Plumbing permits in Bourne

Bourne's Building and Inspection Department handles plumbing and gas inspections, and permit rules vary by job. In general a water heater swap needs a permit pulled by a licensed plumber, and the plumber arranges the inspection. Ask the department about the current steps for your address.
