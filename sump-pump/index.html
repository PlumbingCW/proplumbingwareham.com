When a failed sump pump is an emergency

A sump pump failure is urgent when water is arriving faster than you can hold it back. These are the signs.

  • ✓Water is rising in the sump pit and the pump is silent, humming without moving water or running without stopping.
  • ✓A storm is under way or forecast, and the pump has not cycled even though the pit is full.
  • ✓The basement floor is wet or water is entering at the wall joint, and the pit is not draining.
  • ✓The power is out and you have no battery backup. A pump that cannot run will not protect you.
  • ✓You smell sewage or see a dark color in the pit, which can mean a different problem such as a backed-up line.

Do not walk into standing water if the panel, a furnace or a water heater is nearby. Shut off power at the panel only if you can reach it over dry floor. If water is deep, call us and your electric utility rather than going in.

Why a sump pump matters on this coast

Wareham sits at the head of Buzzards Bay, and the NWS notes that Hurricane Bob in 1991 drove surges of 12 to 15 feet at Onset, Wareham and Bourne. Wareham has four named rivers, including the Wareham and Weweantic, plus long stretches of shoreline. Low lots near the water and along the rivers are where a basement is most likely to see groundwater.

Storms also knock out power, and that is when a plain electric pump stops. During that same storm, more than 60 percent of residents in southeastern Massachusetts and Rhode Island lost power. A pump with a battery backup keeps running when the grid does not.

Common ways a sump pump fails

Most failures are one of these. A plumber can usually tell which from the sound and the pit.

Stuck float switch

The float arm jams on the side of the pit or the tether tangles, so the pump never turns on. This is the most common cause of a dry-running pump.

Burned-out motor

Constant cycling in wet weather, or a pump past its service life, wears the motor out. Humming without pumping is the usual sign.

Failed check valve

Water flows back into the pit after each cycle, so the pump cycles again and again. You hear a thud as water falls back.

Clogged intake or frozen discharge

Silt and gravel plug the intake. In winter, standing water in the discharge line can freeze and block it.

No power

A tripped breaker or outage stops the pump. A battery backup or water-powered backup covers this.

How we repair or replace a sump pump

The job is usually done in one visit once the cause is found.

  1. 1

    Check the pit and power

    We confirm power, test the float and look for debris or a collapsed pit liner.

  2. 2

    Find the failure

    We run the pump with water and watch the float, the motor and the discharge. A check valve is inspected for backflow.

  3. 3

    Fix or replace

    A stuck float or a bad check valve is repaired. If the motor is dead or the pump is old, replacing the pump is the sounder choice.

  4. 4

    Test and explain

    We fill the pit to confirm the pump cycles, then show you how to test it and what a backup adds.

Battery backup and the discharge line

A battery backup pump sits beside the main pump in the pit and runs from a sealed deep-cycle battery when the power fails. It is a second pump, not a spare part, and it needs a battery test every so often. We install and test them.

The discharge line carries water away from the foundation. If it dumps close to the house or runs level, the water can flow back toward the foundation and the pump recycles the same water. A line that runs uphill or ends too near the wall needs rerouting, and an exterior outlet should be set so winter ice does not seal it.

Sump pumps, drains and the town sewer

A sump pump should discharge outdoors. Tying it into a sanitary drain sends clean groundwater into the sewer or septic system, which is a bad use of both. Wareham's Sewer Department runs about 61 miles of gravity pipe and 45 pump stations, so ask the department if you are unsure how your house is connected.

If a basement keeps wetting and no pit exists, a pump alone may not fix it. We explain what we see, and whether a perimeter drain or leak detection is the better next step.
