What a good installation includes

Anyone can set a tank in a corner and connect two hoses. A proper installation also covers the shutoff valves, a new relief valve with a discharge pipe run to a safe spot, a drain pan where the location calls for one, and flexible connectors rated for the job.

For a gas heater, add the gas shutoff, a sediment trap or drip leg where required, a tested gas joint and a vent that rises and slopes correctly. For an electric heater, the circuit has to match the unit's rating, which an older home may not.

Choosing the fuel and type

The right unit depends on what the house already has and how the household uses hot water. These are the main options we talk through.

Natural gas or propane tank

Fast recovery and common in Massachusetts homes. Needs a gas line and a proper vent, so the existing flue condition matters.

Electric tank

Simple venting and flexible placement. Recovery is slower, and it needs the right dedicated circuit.

Heat pump or hybrid

Moves heat instead of making it, so electricity use is lower. Needs space, airflow, a drain and a warm enough room.

Tankless

Heats on demand and frees floor space. Needs the right gas size or heavy electrical service, and we cover it on the tankless water heater page.

Power-vent or condensing

Vents through a wall with PVC and a fan. A good fit when no chimney is available, but it needs a power outlet and a condensate drain.

Sizing a tank to the household

Size is about hot water at the busiest hour, not total tank volume alone. The table pairs typical households with tank sizing logic, and the plumber confirms it against the unit's first-hour rating.

Family of fourBack-to-back showers, laundryA mid-size tank with a good first-hour rating

Permit and inspection in Wareham

Installing or replacing a water heater needs a plumbing permit in Massachusetts. Under 248 CMR 3.05, the permit is issued only to a properly licensed individual and must be in place before the work is installed, and gas fitting needs its own permit too.

Permit timing and requirements are handled through the Wareham Inspectional Services plumbing inspector at Memorial Town Hall, which offers an online permitting portal. By regulation the inspector acts on an application within two working days, though schedules differ, so we plan the install date around the inspection.

Gas or electric in an older Wareham home

The town's housing plan lists a median build year of about 1969, so many homes still carry the wiring and flue of their era. That shapes the choice of unit.

Gas or propane

  • Existing chimney liners may be too large or unlined for a new high-efficiency unit
  • Gas piping sized for the old unit may need an upgrade
  • Venting must be correct, and a carbon monoxide alarm nearby is a sound practice
  • Often the best recovery for a large family

Electric or heat pump

  • Service panels from earlier decades may have no room for a new circuit
  • No flue, so placement is flexible
  • Good for cottages without a gas line
  • A heat pump needs warm space and a condensate drain

Expansion tanks, valves and the small parts that matter

A closed plumbing system, with a check valve or a pressure reducing valve at the meter, has nowhere to send heated water as it expands. A small expansion tank on the cold line absorbs it so the relief valve does not weep and the fittings do not stress.

We also fit new shutoff valves, a dielectric union or brass nipple where dissimilar metals meet, and a tested relief valve discharge pipe. These are small parts, and skipping them is how a new heater ends up leaking in three years.

Seasonal homes and cottages

Wareham has a large seasonal housing stock, from year-round homes to summer cottages near the water. A cottage heater is typically shut down and drained for winter, so the installation should include accessible shutoffs and a drain valve in a spot that makes shutdown easy.

Two separate water districts serve the town, and the town's sewer system does not reach everywhere, so some properties run on septic. Neither changes how the heater is installed, but the water source can change how quickly an anode wears, which is worth asking about on the call.

What to do before the installer arrives

A few steps reduce damage and risk, and they apply whether or not a plumber is on the way.

  • ✓Clear a path and a three-foot work area around the old unit and the new location.
  • ✓Locate the water shutoff and, for gas units, the gas valve at the appliance.
  • ✓Make sure a working carbon monoxide alarm is near the sleeping areas.
  • ✓If you smell gas at any point, leave the house and call the gas utility or 911 first.
