When a commercial plumbing problem is an emergency

A business can usually wait out a dripping tap. It cannot wait out the situations below, because they stop service, risk a health code issue or damage the building.

  • ✓Sewage or gray water is backing up from floor drains, a three-compartment sink or restroom fixtures. Close the kitchen or the restrooms until it is cleared.
  • ✓A burst or leaking supply line is soaking a ceiling, stock or electrical gear. Shut the main valve and keep people away from wet outlets.
  • ✓The grease interceptor or floor sinks are overflowing during service. Stop dish and prep water at once.
  • ✓A flushometer valve on a toilet or urinal will not stop running, or the restrooms have no water at all.
  • ✓Hot water is gone in a food-service space that needs it for dish machines and handwashing.
  • ✓A seasonal or waterfront building has water coming from a pipe that froze or sat unused since fall.

Tell the dispatcher what the business does, how many floors or units are involved and where the shutoffs are. That tells us which tools and parts to load, and it sets the order of repair.

Restaurants, kitchens and grease interceptors

A commercial kitchen sends grease, starch and food solids down the line all day. A grease interceptor, which sits under the floor or in the yard, slows the water so fat floats and solids settle, but once it is full, that waste flows on to the building drain. The usual sign is slow floor drains, a sour smell and sinks that back up at the end of a rush.

We clear the lines with a cable or a hydro jetting nozzle, locate the blockage and tell the owner whether the problem is the building line or the interceptor. Pumping and hauling the interceptor is a separate service, and the town sewer department or the water district can say how often the property needs it.

Fixtures and systems we work on

Commercial plumbing differs from residential work in pipe size, flow and how many people use it at once. These are the systems that fail most often.

Flushometer valves

The diaphragm and the stop inside a commercial toilet or urinal valve wear out. A valve that runs or sticks wastes water and can overflow the bowl.

Floor drains and floor sinks

Strainers clog with debris. A trap that loses its water seal lets odors into the building, so we check priming and trap depth.

Grease interceptors

Baffles, lids and outlet tees fail with age. We inspect the unit's inlet and outlet and clear the lines feeding it.

Backflow preventers

Devices on irrigation, boiler feeds and fire lines keep dirty water from flowing back into the supply. Failed units leak at the relief port.

Water heaters and mixing valves

Food-service spaces need steady hot water. We repair or replace tank and tankless units, and set mixing valves for safe delivery.

Sump and sewage ejector pits

Below-grade restrooms and basements rely on pumps. A failed pump or stuck float means an overflow into the lowest floor.

The Cranberry Highway corridor and Wareham's waterfront

Cranberry Highway is the stretch of US 6 and MA 28 that carries traffic through West Wareham and toward Buzzards Bay and the Cape bridges. Businesses along it range from retail and food service to offices, and depending on the lot a building may be tied to town sewer or run its own septic system. That matters for a restaurant, since a septic system with a busy kitchen needs a different grease plan.

Wareham also sits at the head of Buzzards Bay, and during Hurricane Bob in 1991 the surge at Onset, Bourne, Mashpee and Wareham reached 12 to 15 feet according to the National Weather Service. That history is one reason waterfront and low-lying commercial buildings should have a working sump or ejector pump and a backwater valve on the sewer lateral.

Seasonal businesses: opening and closing checklist

Wareham has a large seasonal housing stock, and many shops, eateries and marina buildings run on the same calendar. Closing and opening the plumbing correctly prevents the callouts that arrive on the first warm weekend.

  • 1Before closing, drain supply lines and fixtures, blow out irrigation and outdoor lines and add non-toxic antifreeze to traps if the building will be unheated.
  • 2At opening, turn the main on slowly with every faucet closed, then walk the building looking for wet spots, dripping stops and weeping unions.
  • 3Run each floor drain and fill each trap with water so sewer gas does not enter.
  • 4Test the water heater, the relief valve and any mixing valve before the first guests arrive.
  • 5Ask for a test of backflow devices on irrigation and boiler feeds, since the local rules for testing vary by water district.

A licensed plumber can walk through the building on a single visit and fix what the checklist finds. That beats finding the weak spot when a pipe lets go in the dining room.

What affects the price of commercial plumbing

A commercial job is priced after the plumber has seen the problem, and you hear the number before work starts. The factors below do most of the moving.

Pipe size and materialLarger lines and cast iron or copper take more labor than a standard residential run.
AccessTight ceilings, finished walls and equipment lines can double the time on a repair.
HoursNight, weekend and holiday callouts follow emergency scheduling, and the number is still quoted first.
Code and permitsReplacing a water heater, backflow device or fixture group requires a permit issued to a licensed plumber under 248 CMR 3.05, and the Wareham Inspectional Services office handles it.
DowntimeSome repairs can be done after closing to protect service hours. Tell us when the space can be reached.

What to do before the plumber arrives

A few steps reduce damage and risk, and they apply whether or not a plumber is on the way.

  • ✓Stop the water to the affected fixture or line at its own shutoff, or at the building main if you cannot find it.
  • ✓Keep staff and customers away from standing water near equipment, outlets and cooking lines.
  • ✓Do not run a floor drain or grease line that is backing up, and do not pour chemicals into it.
  • ✓Write down when it started and what was running, so the plumber can trace the cause faster.
