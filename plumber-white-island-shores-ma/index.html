What the census says about White Island Shores

The 2020 census counted 2,180 residents and 950 housing units in White Island Shores, with 14.9 percent of units vacant. The place covers about 1.3 square miles along White Island Pond.

Vacant in census terms includes homes used only part of the year, so a share of the housing may sit closed for stretches. That is a hint, not a count of seasonal homes.

For a plumbing call, the numbers lead to one useful question. Is the house lived in all year or used part of the year? Seasonal homes need winter shutdown and spring turn-on, and year-round homes need frost protection and regular checks.

How to find out what your house is connected to

We do not assume anything about the water or waste setup on a given street. These steps settle it.

  1. 1

    Look at your water bill

    A bill from the Wareham Fire District or the Onset Fire District means public water. No bill usually means a private well.

  2. 2

    Look for the water meter

    A meter near the entry point points to public water.

  3. 3

    Ask about sewer

    The Wareham Sewer Department at 508-295-6144 can say whether your street is sewered.

  4. 4

    Look for a septic lid

    A tank lid or a distribution box in the yard means septic. Ask the seller or the Board of Health for the records.

Public water or a well: what changes

The two setups call for different checks.

Public water

  • Water pressure comes from the district, and a regulator may protect the house.
  • A leak before the meter is usually the district's concern, and one after the meter is yours.
  • Your bill shows the usage, which helps trace a hidden leak.

A private well

  • A pump and a pressure tank supply the house.
  • A power outage can also mean no water.
  • Water testing is the owner's decision.

Seasonal use and frost

If the house is closed for part of the year, these are the jobs to plan.

Winter shutdown

Close the main, drain the lines and protect the heater. See our water heater repair page for heater problems after a freeze.

Spring turn-on

Open the main slowly and watch the meter before opening any faucet. See leak detection if the meter keeps moving.

Sump pump

If you have a pit, test the pump before spring rains. See sump pump service.

Drain smells

A dry trap lets sewer gas in. Pour water into each drain after a long closure.

Permits and licenses in Wareham

A permit is required under 248 CMR 3.05 before plumbing work is installed, altered, replaced or repaired, with exceptions for clearing a stoppage and for leak repairs in a faucet or valve. Permits are issued to licensed individuals through Inspectional Services.

Check the license number in the state's Check a License tool before you hire.
