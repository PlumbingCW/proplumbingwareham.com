When a burst pipe is an emergency: shut it down first

Any pipe that is spraying, pouring or soaking a ceiling is an emergency. Do these in order, and keep your phone with you while you work.

  1. 1

    Find the main shutoff

    It is usually where the water line enters the house, often in a basement, a crawlspace, a utility closet or beside the water heater. The valve is either a round wheel or a lever. Turn a wheel clockwise until it stops, and turn a lever a quarter turn so it sits across the pipe.

  2. 2

    Drain the lines

    Open the lowest cold faucet in the house, then the highest one, and flush a toilet. This releases pressure and pulls standing water out of the broken section.

  3. 3

    Cut power to wet areas

    If water is near outlets, light fixtures, a panel or appliances, turn off the circuit at the panel, but only if you can reach it without stepping in water. If you cannot, stay out and tell the dispatcher.

  4. 4

    Shut off the water heater

    Turn the gas valve to off or the electric breaker to off. A tank with no incoming water can overheat or damage its elements.

  5. 5

    Protect what you can

    Move furniture and valuables, lay towels, and photograph every wet surface for your insurance claim. Then call the 24/7 line if you have not already.

What actually makes a pipe burst

Freezing is the textbook cause, since water expands as it turns to ice and the pressure builds between the ice plug and a closed faucet. The split usually forms in the pipe wall next to the ice, not at the ice, which is why a thawed line can seem fine and then pour water an hour later.

Corrosion is the other big cause. Old galvanized steel rusts from the inside and thins until a spot gives way, and copper can develop pinholes that grow into a spray. Water hammer from a fast-closing valve, a failed fitting and a pipe nicked during a past repair also account for many calls.

Seasonal homes and the first spring turn-on

Wareham's housing plan notes a large seasonal housing stock, and cottages that were not fully drained in the fall are the ones most likely to split a line over the winter. Because the water is off, nobody sees the damage until the main is opened in spring, and then the leak runs into a closed house.

If you are reopening a cottage, open the main slowly and check the meter and every wall and ceiling before you leave. A split pipe found on day one is a repair. The same split found two weeks later can mean wet insulation, mold and a floor that needs replacing.

How a burst pipe visit goes

Once the plumber arrives, the order of work is the same for most homes.

  • 1Confirm the water is off and confirm the main valve works. A stuck or leaking valve gets replaced so you have a reliable shutoff.
  • 2Open the damaged area just enough to see the split and the pipe on either side of it.
  • 3Cut back to sound pipe, not to the edge of the visible damage, because weakened pipe next to a split often fails again.
  • 4Make the repair, restore water slowly and look for air, hammer and new leaks at every joint.
  • 5Test the line under pressure, tell you what was found, and leave you with notes on any other pipe that looks suspect.

If the pipe is wet inside a wall, the plumber stops at the pipe. Patching drywall, repainting and drying out the structure belong to a drywall or restoration contractor, and your insurer may have its own list.

Frozen but not yet burst

If a faucet is dripping nothing and a pipe is cold and frosty, you may have time. Open the faucet, wrap the pipe with towels soaked in warm water or aim a hair dryer on low at the frozen section, and work from the faucet back toward the frozen spot. Never use a torch or open flame, which has started house fires and can damage a joint.

If you see a bulge, a crack or water where there was none, stop thawing and close the main. Call right away, because a pipe that has already split is better repaired on your schedule than discovered at 3 a.m. The same crew can pair the repair with leak detection if you suspect a second break.

Permits and the repair itself

Most plumbing work needs a permit in Massachusetts under 248 CMR 3.05, and permits go only to licensed plumbers. The rules do allow a licensee to make an emergency repair first, as long as tests are done, the inspector is notified, and inspection happens no later than the next working day. Wareham's Inspectional Services at 54 Marion Road handles plumbing permits, and the plumber takes care of the paperwork.

If the burst traces back to a cracked line outside the house, water line repair is the service that applies. Dispatch also covers Bourne and Middleborough, so the nearest open plumber may not be in Wareham.
