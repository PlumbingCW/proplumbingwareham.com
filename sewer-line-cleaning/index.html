Main sewer backup: what to do first

A main line blockage gets worse with every gallon you send down it. Work through these steps while you wait for the plumber.

  • ✓Stop using every drain. That includes the dishwasher, washing machine, showers and toilets.
  • ✓Do not pour caustic drain cleaner into a toilet or floor drain. It will not reach a blockage in a large pipe and it makes the next step dangerous.
  • ✓Keep children and pets out of the basement if water is rising from a floor drain, because backed-up wastewater carries bacteria.
  • ✓Move belongings off the floor if you can do so safely, and leave anything near wet outlets alone.
  • ✓Note which fixtures are affected and the lowest one that is backing up. Tell the dispatcher, because that helps pick the right machine.

On a septic system, do not call a pumping truck before the pipe is checked. A backup that starts at the toilet can be a house line blockage and the tank may be fine.

Sewer line versus drain line: where the work happens

A drain line serves one fixture or a group of them inside the house. Your sewer line, sometimes called the main drain or lateral, is the larger pipe, commonly four inches across, that carries everything out and under the yard. A plain kitchen or bath clog is a drain cleaning job, while a whole-house backup belongs to this page.

Wareham has a large seasonal housing stock, and the town housing plan describes dense summer communities. A cottage that was closed for months can have a main line that dried out and collected scale, and the first full weekend of use is when it clogs.

What blocks a main line

The cause tells us which tool to use and whether the clog will come back.

Tree roots

Roots enter at a joint and grow into a mat. A cutting head opens the pipe, and without a seal at the joint they return.

Grease buildup

Cooking fats cool in the pipe, layer with paper and solids, and narrow the bore over years.

Flushed wipes and paper

Wipes labeled flushable do not break down. They snag on rough pipe walls and bind into a rope.

Scale in cast iron

Older cast iron gets a rough crust inside. It catches waste and slowly closes the opening.

Sags and bellies

A low spot in the buried pipe holds water and solids. The clog returns until the pipe is repaired.

The tools we use on a main line

For a standard main line we use a sectional cable machine or a large drum machine with a cutting head chosen for the blockage. A spear head punches through a soft clog, a spiral head reaches roots and a chain cutter scrapes the pipe wall. The cable goes in through a cleanout or, if none exists, a toilet flange or roof vent.

When the cable is not enough, we step up to hydro jetting, which sends high-pressure water through a jetter nozzle and scours the pipe wall. Then a camera shows what the pipe looks like, which answers whether the line needs sewer line repair or only maintenance.

Town sewer or septic: whose pipe is it

Wareham's Sewer Department runs about 61 miles of gravity pipe and 45 pump stations, and areas north of Route 25 could not be added to the system, according to the town housing plan. If your home connects to a town main, a blockage can sit in your own lateral or in the street main, and the plumber can usually tell which from where the cable stops.

Once the pipe is on the town side, the Sewer Department is the right contact. If you are on septic, a main line clog may be in the house line, the inlet baffle of the tank or the pipe leading out of it, so we check that before any pumping is recommended.

Preventing the next backup

Keep wipes, paper towels, feminine products and cooking grease out of the drain, even if the package says flushable. Plant trees well away from the line, because most root intrusion starts at a joint within reach of mature roots.

If your main line has backed up more than once, ask for a camera look at the same visit. Repeat clogs are usually a symptom, and a sewer inspection pins down what the symptom is. The same crews also serve Rochester and Bourne when a backup call comes in.

Permit rules for line cleaning

Clearing a stoppage in a sewer line falls under the no-permit list in 248 CMR 3.05(1)(c), so cleaning can go ahead quickly. Digging up and replacing pipe is different, because 248 CMR 3.05 requires a permit before that work starts, and the plumber pulls it.
