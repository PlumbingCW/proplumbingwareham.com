When a backed-up line calls for jetting right away

Not every clog needs a jetter. These signs say the line needs more than another snake.

  • ✓A cable went in, the drain cleared, and it clogged again within a few weeks.
  • ✓The previous plumber said the cable stuck or stopped partway down the line.
  • ✓A restaurant-style grease problem at home, with white or gray fat coating every cleanout you open.
  • ✓Roots are known to be in the line, and you need the pipe wall cleaned after they were cut.
  • ✓The main line is fully blocked and sewage is coming up in the lowest drain. Call the 24/7 line first, then stop all water use.

If the pipe is cracked, collapsed or has loose joints, pressure can make a bad situation worse. That is why we camera before we jet.

How hydro jetting works

A hydro jetter is a pump that sends water at high pressure through a hose with a nozzle on the end. Jets on the nozzle push it forward and send other jets backward, which pulls the hose along the pipe and scours the wall in a full circle. Different nozzle styles cut roots, break up grease or clear scale.

A cable machine, by contrast, bores a hole through the middle of a clog and leaves the coating on the pipe wall. That is why a jetted line stays clear longer. For plain cabling, see sewer line cleaning, and for fixture clogs inside the house, drain cleaning.

Jetting versus snaking

Both tools have a place. We choose by what the camera shows.

Cable and snake

  • Good for first-time clogs, soft blockages and fixture lines.
  • Quick to set up and works through small cleanouts.
  • Leaves grease and scale on the pipe wall.
  • Can snag on an offset or a broken joint.

Hydro jetting

  • Good for repeat clogs, heavy grease and root-fouled main lines.
  • Scours the whole inside of the pipe, not just the center.
  • Needs a cleanout and a sound pipe to be safe.
  • Not suited to cracked or collapsed pipe.

What a jetting visit looks like

The sequence matters. Skipping the camera is the common way jetting causes harm.

  1. 1

    Camera the line

    We look at the pipe condition first. Cracks, offsets or fragile old fiber pipe change the plan.

  2. 2

    Set up at a cleanout

    The jetter hose goes in through a cleanout. If none exists, a toilet flange or a roof vent can work.

  3. 3

    Jet in passes

    We work from the blockage outward, choosing a nozzle for the cause and adjusting pressure to the pipe.

  4. 4

    Camera again

    A second run shows the clean pipe wall. You see whether roots, scale or damage remain.

Which lines in Wareham benefit most

The town housing plan puts the median home at about 1969 construction, with roughly one in five houses built before 1939. Septic systems and jetting

If your house sends waste to a septic tank, the jetter clears the pipe between the house and the tank, and it does not treat the tank or the leach field. Massachusetts Title 5 covers septic inspections at a sale, and only MassDEP-approved inspectors can do them.

Camera work from a plumber is different from that report. A sewer camera inspection shows the pipe, while Title 5 looks at the whole system.

Does jetting need a permit?

Clearing a stoppage is one of the jobs 248 CMR 3.05(1)(c) lists as not needing a plumbing permit, so a jetting visit can happen without waiting on paperwork. If the camera then shows a pipe that must be cut out and replaced, that repair is permit work handled by the licensed plumber.
