Plumbing Installation in Wareham, MA

Plumbing installation covers anything that adds or moves pipes and fixtures: a new vanity and toilet, a dishwasher or washing machine hookup, a sink relocated across the room, or the rough-in for a bathroom or kitchen. Licensed plumbers handle the work, and the price is clear before anything is cut.

We install in Wareham, Onset, East Wareham and West Wareham, and we schedule same-day appointments when available for simple fixture swaps. Larger jobs get a site visit and a written scope first.

What counts as plumbing installation

The term covers a wide span of work, so it helps to separate three levels. A fixture swap replaces one faucet, toilet or sink in the same spot using the existing supply and drain. A hookup connects an appliance, such as a dishwasher, ice maker or washer, to lines that are already in the wall.

A new run is a different job. It adds supply lines, a drain, a vent and sometimes a shutoff to a place that had none, as in a basement bath, a second kitchen sink or a laundry room moved upstairs. Each level has its own tools, time and permit question.

Common installation jobs

These are the jobs people call about most, with what each one involves.

New toilet or vanity

Remove the old fixture, check the flange and stops, set the new one on a fresh ring and test for leaks. A new closet flange or supply stop is often part of the job.

Dishwasher hookup

A hot-water supply with a small valve, a drain hose looped high under the counter or tied to an air gap, and a 120-volt circuit supplied by an electrician.

Washing machine box

Two supply valves and a standpipe with a trap. The drain should accept the machine's pumped flow without backing up.

Sink relocation

Moving a sink means moving its supply, drain and vent. The farther it moves, the more floor and wall are opened.

Bathroom rough-in

The drain, vent and supply are set inside open walls and floor before tile and drywall. Fixture positions are measured from framing.

Kitchen rough-in

Sink, dishwasher, disposal and ice maker are positioned before cabinets. Gas lines for ranges are handled by a licensed gas fitter under separate rules.

How an installation job runs

Most jobs follow the same order, from the first phone call to the final test.

  1. 1

    Tell us what is being installed, the age of the house and whether the walls are open. Photos of the existing fixtures and shutoffs help.

  2. 2

    The plumber checks the existing supply and drain, the venting and access. For a basic swap this is the same visit as the work.

  3. 3

    Price and permit

    You get a clear price before work starts. If a permit is needed, the licensed plumber applies for it before the pipes are touched.

  4. 4

    Install and rough-in

    Fixtures are set, lines connected and, for new runs, pipes are pressure tested while the walls are open.

  5. 5

    Inspection and finish

    For permitted work, the plumbing inspector checks the rough-in or the finished job. Tile and drywall wait until it passes.

Permits and licensed plumbers in Wareham

Under 248 CMR 3.05, a permit must be issued before plumbing or gas work is installed, altered, removed, replaced or repaired. Permits go only to properly licensed individuals, not apprentices, and an application is made in writing before work begins. Wareham's Inspectional Services office at Memorial Town Hall on Marion Road handles building, electrical, plumbing and gas permits.

Exceptions exist. Repairing a leak in a faucet, a valve or the working part of a fixture, and clearing a stoppage, need no permit under 3.05(1)(c). New installations, relocated lines and water heater replacements do. A homeowner who hires an unlicensed worker can end up with work that fails inspection, so you can check any plumber's license through the state's license lookup on mass.gov.

Wareham homes: what to expect behind the walls

The median Wareham home was built in 1969, and about a fifth of the housing predates 1939. Houses of that age often have galvanized steel supply lines that have narrowed inside, cast iron drains and vents that were sized for fewer fixtures. A new bathroom or sink can reveal that the old line cannot feed it well.

In those cases a short section of pipe is replaced, or the plan expands into repiping a branch or the whole house. We tell you when that is the honest route and when a simple tie-in will do.

Cottages, additions and conversions

Wareham has a large seasonal housing stock, and many summer cottages are now used year-round. A conversion adds bathrooms, laundry and heat, which is where installation work piles up. Supply lines, hot water capacity and drain slope all have to be checked, because a cottage plumbed for weekends does not always handle a full-time household.

A new water heater for the extra load follows water heater installation rules, including the permit. Sewer-connected homes tie into the town's gravity sewer. Homes on septic need the tank, the distribution box and the leach field checked for the added flow, and Title 5 rules apply if the property is sold.

Before you buy fixtures

A few checks before the order goes in save a second trip.

  • ✓Measure the rough-in distance from the wall to the center of the toilet drain. Most are 12 inches, but older houses vary.
  • ✓Confirm the sink cutout size and the number of faucet holes in the countertop.
  • ✓Keep the manufacturer's installation sheet, because the plumber needs the supply and drain connections.
  • ✓Check that the shutoff valves under the old fixture work. If they do not, plan to replace them.
  • ✓Ask whether a toilet repair would solve the problem instead of a new bowl.

If something is leaking in the meantime, close the shutoff and call. A leak is fixed first.
