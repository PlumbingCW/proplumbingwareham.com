Who owns the problem: town system or your pipe

Middleborough's open space plan says town water and sewer serve the center of town and some spurs off it, and most of the town is on private septic and wells. Before anyone starts digging, it helps to know whose pipe is leaking.

Town water in the center, wells beyond it

The Middleborough Water Department draws only on groundwater, and its page says the system has about 130 miles of main and 800 hydrants. Public supply began in 1885, and the department notes that its infrastructure varies in age. Older mains and older service lines are where leaks tend to start.

The water is pH-adjusted with potassium hydroxide, and the department's page lists notices on PFAS and manganese. Check that page for the current status on your street. Emergency dispatch is available around the clock, so a leak that shows up on a Sunday does not have to wait.

Before we arrive: if you have a burst pipe or active leak

The steps depend on if the house is on town water or a well. Do the ones that fit your house.

Shut the main valve

On town water, close the main where the line enters the house, often near the meter. On a well, close it near the pressure tank. Open a low faucet afterward to drain the line.

Cut power to wet areas

On a well, switch off the pump breaker. For any wet room, switch off its circuit at the panel only when the floor around the panel is dry.

Clear the area

Move people, pets and valuables away from standing water. Keep out of a flooded cellar if the furnace or panel is down there.

Call the 24/7 line

Tell the dispatcher your street, the nearest cross street and whether the house has town sewer or septic.

Sewer center, septic everywhere else

A 2015 hazard plan put about 31 percent of residents on public sewer, and the town runs its own wastewater treatment plant. If your street is sewered, a blockage can be in your lateral or in the town main, and the first job is working out which.

A camera head on a push cable shows the pipe, and a sewer camera inspection often settles the question. Roots and grease are common causes, and drain cleaning with a drum cable opens most of them. If the pipe is cracked or offset, sewer line repair follows the camera findings.

Older houses around the center

Surviving houses go back to the 1750s, and the center has Gothic Revival cottages from the 1860s and Queen Anne and Colonial Revival homes from the 1890s on. The state survey and the town's walking guide list many South Main Street houses built between the 1770s and the early 1900s.

A house that old has often been replumbed in pieces. You may find copper, galvanized steel and plastic on one run, and the joints between them are where leaks and low pressure turn up. If it has several pipe types, a plumber can propose a staged repiping plan.

Permits, step by step

The Building Department at 20 Center Street handles plumbing and gas permits, and the town runs online permitting. Here is how a heater swap or remodel goes.

  1. 1

    Hire a licensed plumber

    Work must be done by a Massachusetts-licensed plumber or gas fitter.

  2. 2

    File before work starts

    The town's fee schedule says starting without a permit raises the fee, so the permit goes in first.

  3. 3

    Pair the permits

    A water heater carries a plumbing permit and a gas permit, filed together when it is a domestic gas unit.

  4. 4

    Book the inspection

    The town inspects the work. A failed inspection means a re-inspection fee, so we check connections before we leave.

Roads, routes and the rotary

I-495 runs through town with four interchanges, and Routes 28, 18, 79, 105 and 44 cross it. The town's own planning documents mention congestion at the rotary and I-495, and traffic can add to the drive.

So we ask for your street, your cross street and the route you are nearest to. That helps dispatch pick the right plumber, and it tells you the route you may want to avoid if you need to leave the house.

After the 2010 floods

Two 100-year floods hit in March and April 2010, and the town's hazard plan records flooded approaches to bridges on Summer Street and Titicut Street. The Nemasket River, which begins at Lake Assawompset, flooded severely in 2010.

Homes near that water can see sump pumps run constantly for days. Sump pump service before the wet season, with a working check valve and a clear discharge line, keeps a cellar dry.

Lakeville and the towns around Middleborough

Lakeville, ZIP 02347, split from Middleborough in 1853 and borders it. We reach it with the same 24/7 dispatch, along with Carver 02330, Rochester 02770 and Plympton 02367.

Lakeville homes lean on private wells and septic, and the Assawompset Pond Complex lies partly in town. A flooded cellar, a failed pump or a backup there is the same emergency call.

State rule behind the permit counter

Whatever the town's counter asks for, the state rule is 248 CMR 3.05. Clearing a stoppage and repairing a leak in a faucet or valve are exempt, and most other plumbing work needs a permit issued to a licensed plumber before it starts, with an inspector reviewing it afterward.
