When a boiler problem is an emergency

Safety comes first, then heat. Work through the list in order and call the 24/7 line once you are safe.

  • ✓You smell gas, or a carbon monoxide alarm sounds. Leave the building with everyone inside, call your gas utility or 911 from outside, and do not turn lights or switches on or off. We come after the utility says it is safe.
  • ✓There is heavy water around the boiler or a pipe is spraying. Close the cold water feed to the boiler and shut the power at the switch if the floor around it is dry.
  • ✓The boiler is making loud banging or a steady hissing, or the pressure gauge is climbing into the red. Turn the boiler off at its service switch.
  • ✓Oil is on the floor near an oil-fired unit. Shut off the burner switch and keep flames and sparks away.
  • ✓The house has no heat in freezing weather. Frozen pipes can follow within hours in a cold, empty building.

Night, weekend and holiday calls go to the same 24/7 line, and we offer same-day appointments when a slot is open. Say whether the boiler burns oil or gas when you call.

What the boiler is telling you

The way a boiler fails points to the cause. These are the symptoms we hear and what is usually behind them.

No heat, boiler is cold

A tripped power switch, a failed thermostat, a dead circulator, a low-water cutoff that has locked out, or a burner that will not ignite.

Boiler runs but rooms stay cold

Air in the loops, a stuck zone valve, a failing circulator pump or a closed valve.

Pressure too high or too low

A failed expansion tank or a leak. A relief valve that drips is warning you, so leave the discharge open.

Banging, kettling or gurgling

Scale or sludge in a hydronic boiler, trapped air or a pump moving too much water.

Water on the floor

A weeping fitting, a failing relief valve, a pinhole in the heat exchanger or a leaking circulator flange.

Short cycling

Oversized boiler, a bad sensor or a clogged flue. It wears the burner out quickly.

How we diagnose a boiler

The first step is simple, which is to find out whether the boiler is getting what it needs. We check the thermostat call for heat, the power and emergency switch, the water level and pressure, and the fuel supply.

After that comes the working parts. A plumber looks at the circulator, the zone valves, the expansion tank and the relief valve. On a gas unit the ignition, flame sensing and venting are checked too, and on an oil unit the burner, nozzle and filter are part of the burner service. If the fault sits outside what we repair, we say so and tell you what kind of tech to call.

Safe checks while you wait

These checks are safe for most owners. Stop right away if you smell gas or oil, hear a hiss or see scorching.

  1. 1

    Check the thermostat

    Make sure it is set to heat and above the room temperature. New batteries solve more calls than people expect.

  2. 2

    Look at the power switch

    There is usually a service switch near the boiler or at the top of the cellar stairs. A kicked switch is an easy fix.

  3. 3

    Read the pressure gauge

    A cold hydronic boiler typically sits around the manufacturer's stated range. Do not add water if the number is high.

  4. 4

    Check the fuel

    For oil, look at the tank gauge. For gas, confirm other gas appliances work.

  5. 5

    Note the display

    Write down any code or blinking light for the plumber.

Gas, oil and carbon monoxide

Gas and oil boilers produce exhaust that must go outside. A cracked flue, a blocked chimney or a failed vent fan can push carbon monoxide into the living space, and a working CO alarm on each level of the home is the best protection.

In Massachusetts, gas piping and fittings fall under the Fuel Gas and Plumbing Code, 248 CMR 10.00, and gas work needs a licensed professional. If you notice a gas smell, we will not ask you to troubleshoot it. Leave first, call the utility, then call us.

Wareham heating, cottages and power outages

Wareham has a large seasonal housing stock, and a cottage that heats only on weekends needs a plan for freezing weather. A boiler that is shut off with water in the system can burst pipes, so drain-down or a low-temperature setting should be a decision, not an accident. A frozen line after a cold snap is a job for burst pipe repair.

Boilers also need electricity for the circulator, controls and burner. Hurricane Bob in 1991 left more than 60 percent of residents in southeast Massachusetts and southeast Rhode Island without power, and any long outage can stop heat even when the fuel is fine.
