When a garbage disposal is an emergency

A slow disposal can wait until morning. These signs mean the sink, the cabinet or the kitchen drain is already in trouble.

  • ✓Water is dripping or pouring from the bottom or the side of the unit into the cabinet. Turn the disposal off, shut the sink stops and put a bucket under it.
  • ✓The sink backs up when the disposal runs, and the dishwasher drain hose connects to the same fitting. Stop running the dishwasher.
  • ✓The unit hums and does not turn, trips the reset button repeatedly or smells hot. Unplug it or switch off the circuit before anyone reaches in.
  • ✓Food and gray water are coming back up into the other sink basin. That points to a blockage past the disposal.
  • ✓Water has reached the outlet, the switch or the wiring under the sink. Cut power at the panel only if the floor in front of it is dry, then call.

Never put a hand or a utensil into a disposal that has power. A jammed unit can restart without warning when the reset button is pressed.

Why disposals jam, hum and leak

A disposal is a small motor above a grinding plate with two swiveling impellers. It does not use blades. When a fibrous peel, a utensil or a pit lands between the plate and the wall, the motor stalls and hums. Turning the flywheel from underneath with the hex wrench that came with the unit often frees it.

Leaks come from three places. The top flange gasket, the dishwasher inlet and the lower housing seam each fail for different reasons. A leak from the lower seam means the housing is cracked or the internal seal has worn, and the unit has to be replaced.

Safe steps before the plumber arrives

Follow these in order. They take only a wrench, a flashlight and a bucket.

  1. 1

    Power off

    Flip the wall switch off, then unplug the cord under the sink if there is one. A hardwired unit is switched off at its breaker.

  2. 2

    Look for the cause

    Shine a flashlight into the opening. Pull any visible object out with tongs, never with fingers.

  3. 3

    Turn the flywheel

    Insert the hex wrench into the socket at the center bottom and work it back and forth until it spins freely. Then press the red reset button.

  4. 4

    Check the leak path

    Dry the unit and cabinet, run a little water and see where it drips. A leak at the top is a gasket, a leak at the side is the dishwasher hose.

  5. 5

    Stop at the clog

    If the sink fills with water and does not drain, the blockage is farther down. Stop and call.

Choosing a replacement unit

If the old disposal has to go, the new one should match the sink and how the household cooks.

HorsepowerA larger motor handles fibrous food and runs quieter. Half horsepower suits light use, and families who cook often do better with more.
Mounting styleMany sinks use a three-bolt mount that needs a specific ring. We bring the common ones and check yours.
Batch feed or continuous feedBatch models run only with the stopper in place. They suit homes with small children.
Dishwasher connectionThe knockout plug in the dishwasher inlet has to be removed, or the dishwasher backs up into the sink.
ElectricalThe unit needs an outlet or a hardwired box. Any new circuit work is a separate item.

A replacement is handled under plumbing installation, so the permit question gets answered before we start.

Sewer, septic and the disposal

Wareham's sewer department reports roughly 61 miles of gravity sewer and 45 pump stations, but the town's housing plan notes that areas north of Route 25 could not be added. A kitchen on a septic system treats a disposal differently. Ground food adds solids to the tank, which fill sooner and can carry fats into the leach field.

Massachusetts requires a Title 5 inspection when a septic property is sold. A tank that has been loaded by a disposal can fail the inspection or need pumping sooner, so septic households should use the unit lightly or take it out.

Grease, clogs and the drain beyond the disposal

Most disposals do not fail because of the unit. They fail because the drain behind them is partly blocked, so water has no place to go and rises into the other basin. Fat that cooled in the trap is the common culprit, and drain cleaning with a cable clears it. A camera helps if the problem returns.

In a cottage opened for the season, the first week of use is when stored grease and dried food residue loosen. Run cold water for 15 seconds after each use to move waste past the trap, and keep bones, rice, pasta, coffee grounds and fibrous peels out of the unit.
