Installing an Under Sink Water Filtration System: A Wareham Guide

An under sink filter is a canister or membrane unit that connects to the cold supply under the kitchen sink and sends filtered water to a dedicated faucet. Installation takes a couple of hours: shut off the cold supply, add a feed adapter, mount the housing, run tubing to the faucet, flush the cartridges and check for leaks.

This guide covers which filter type to pick for your water, the install steps, and the reasons a licensed plumber is the safer choice for a faucet hole in a stone countertop or a supply change.

By the Wareham Pro Plumbing team | Published and updated October 24, 2026

Start with the water, not the filter

The right filter depends on what you want removed, and that depends on where your water comes from. Wareham is served by two districts, the Wareham Fire District and the Onset Fire District, and many homes in town and in the surrounding towns draw from private wells.

Ask your district for its annual water quality report before buying anything. That report lists what was detected and at what level, and it is the only reliable basis for choosing between a basic carbon filter and a more targeted system.

What the two Wareham districts say about their water

The Wareham Fire District pumps from seven gravel-packed wells 60 to 90 feet deep in the Plymouth-Carver aquifer, adds lime to adjust pH, and uses sodium hypochlorite as the disinfectant at the wellfields. The Onset Fire District draws from four groundwater wells and uses sodium hydroxide for pH and corrosion control. A carbon filter handles chlorine taste and odor, but your district's report is the place to check anything else.

Source type: Wareham Fire District and Onset Fire District

Tools and parts to gather

Most kits include the housing, tubing and a feed adapter. You will still need a few items from the shop.

  • ✓Adjustable wrench and a screwdriver set
  • ✓Teflon tape and a bucket with towels
  • ✓Drill with a bit sized for a new faucet hole, if the sink or counter lacks one
  • ✓Tubing cutter or sharp utility knife
  • ✓Cartridge wrench that fits your housing
  • ✓Flashlight and a way to mark stud or cabinet-wall positions

Check the cabinet first. The canisters need clearance below to drop the housing off when the cartridge is changed, and the dishwasher supply and drain lines often take up that space.

Installing the system in seven steps

Read the manual for your specific unit. These steps cover the typical order for a standard kit.

  1. 1

    Shut off the cold water

    Close the cold angle stop under the sink, then open the kitchen faucet to relieve pressure.

  2. 2

    Mount the housing

    Hold the bracket against the cabinet wall, mark the screw holes and fasten into solid backing so the full housing weight is supported.

  3. 3

    Fit the feed adapter

    Disconnect the cold supply line from the stop, thread on the adapter with Teflon tape, then reconnect the line. Hand tighten and add a quarter turn with a wrench.

  4. 4

    Install the filter faucet

    If you need a new hole, drill carefully through the sink deck. Stone and porcelain can crack, so a plumber should do those.

  5. 5

    Run the tubing

    Push tube ends fully into push-fit fittings. Keep the runs short, without kinks.

  6. 6

    Flush the cartridges

    Open the stop slowly, run water through the filter faucet for the time listed in the manual, and release trapped air.

  7. 7

    Check for leaks

    Dry every fitting with a paper towel, wait ten minutes, then check again. Recheck the next day.

Private wells: test first

Homes outside the district mains, including many in Carver and Rochester, rely on private wells with pressure tanks. Nobody monitors a private well for you, so water testing is your job. A state certified lab can test for bacteria, nitrates, metals and other items, and the result tells you what to filter.

A filter cannot replace disinfection if bacteria are present. If a test comes back positive, treat the well first and then add filtration under the sink. Power failures also stop a well pump, so keep a few days of drinking water on hand.

Under sink filter, whole house system or pitcher

Each has a role. Choose by what problem you are solving and how many taps need treatment.

Under sink filter

  • Treats the drinking and cooking tap only
  • Installs in a couple of hours at a single cabinet
  • Dedicated faucet gives filtered water on demand
  • Cartridge changes are quick and low cost per gallon

Whole house system

  • Treats every tap, shower and appliance
  • Sits at the main line and needs a plumber
  • Handles sediment, iron or scale across the house
  • Larger upfront job that involves cutting the main supply

When to hire a licensed plumber

Call a plumber if the shutoff valve is stuck or corroded, if the supply line is galvanized or in poor shape, or if you need a faucet hole in granite, quartz or porcelain. Reverse osmosis units also need a drain saddle on the sink trap, which should be done right to avoid a slow leak in the cabinet.

A plumbing installation visit covers the filter, faucet and supply fittings, and we explain the setup and give a clear price first. If your pressure drops or you find a wet cabinet after install, a water line repair or a quick leak detection check finds the source.

Keeping the system working after install

Write the install date on the housing with a marker and set a phone reminder for the first cartridge change. A spent carbon block stops removing chlorine taste but still passes water, so you may not notice it has quit.

Once a year, shut the stop, release the pressure at the filter faucet and look for drips at every push-fit connection. Replace O-rings in the housing whenever you change the cartridges, and wipe them with the food-safe lubricant the maker supplies. A cabinet that smells musty is the early sign of a slow leak.

Permits and the code

Under 248 CMR 3.05, a permit is generally required before plumbing is installed, altered or replaced, and it is pulled by a licensed plumber. Changing a cartridge or repairing a leak at a fitting does not need one. Ask Wareham inspectional services if your project changes the supply piping.

What to do before installing a filter

A few steps reduce damage and risk, and they apply whether or not a plumber is on the way.

  • ✓Turn off the cold supply at the stop under the sink and open the faucet to release pressure.
  • ✓Clear the cabinet, lay a towel down and keep a small bucket under the work area.
  • ✓Do not drill through a countertop or cabinet without checking for hidden supply and drain lines.
  • ✓Run the system and check every fitting for drips before closing the cabinet, then again the next day.
