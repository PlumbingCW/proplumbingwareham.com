What Does a New Garbage Disposal Cost to Install in Wareham?

The cost of a new garbage disposal and its installation depends on five things: the unit's horsepower, whether it is a continuous-feed or batch-feed model, what electrical setup is under the sink, whether a dishwasher drains through it, and what the drain pipe looks like. A straight swap of a similar unit under an existing outlet is the short end. A first-time install with new wiring is the long end.

This page lists each price driver in plain terms, with no made-up figures, so you can describe your sink accurately on the phone and get a clear price before any work starts.

  • Horsepower and feed type
  • Outlet and switch
  • Dishwasher knockout

What moves the price of a disposal install

The unit itself is only part of the bill. Labor changes with what is already under the sink, and an ordinary swap is a different job from a new install.

HorsepowerCommon sizes run from about one-third horsepower to one horsepower. Bigger motors cost more, run quieter and grind harder, and they are heavier to hang on an old sink flange.
Feed typeA continuous-feed unit runs while a wall switch is on. A batch-feed unit starts only when a stopper lid is twisted in place, so it costs more but suits homes with children.
ElectricalA disposal needs power at the sink base. If there is no outlet or switched circuit, an electrician must add one, and that is a separate trade and often a separate permit.
Dishwasher connectionThe dishwasher drain hose attaches to a small inlet on the disposal. New units have a plug knocked into it that must be removed, or the dishwasher will back up.
Drain and trapRusted or mismatched sink flange, a leaking trap or an old cast-iron stub adds parts and time to the job.

Brand, noise insulation and a sound-reduced motor mount also change the unit price. A clear price is quoted before work starts, based on what you describe and what the plumber sees on arrival.

Horsepower: how much is enough

A one-third or one-half horsepower unit handles scraps from a household that cooks a few meals at home. Three-quarters and one horsepower models spin faster, jam less and handle tougher scraps like small bones and fibrous vegetables, though no disposal replaces a trash can.

A larger motor does not clear a bad drain. If water backs up through the unit, the cause is usually a clogged trap or branch line, not the disposal, and a plumbing installation visit can include correcting an undersized or flat drain run at the same time.

Continuous feed or batch feed

Both types grind food the same way. The difference is how they start, and that choice changes cost, wiring and safety.

Continuous feed

  • Starts with a wall switch or an air-switch button.
  • Common, lower unit price and widely stocked.
  • Lets you feed scraps while the unit runs.
  • Needs a switched outlet or hardwired circuit under the sink.

Batch feed

  • Starts only when the stopper lid is twisted into the flange.
  • Higher unit price and fewer models.
  • Keeps hands and utensils away from the grinder.
  • Often avoids a wall switch, which can save wiring when none exists.

What an installation visit looks like

A swap takes less time than most homeowners expect, but the order matters. Each step below protects a joint or a circuit that is easy to get wrong.

  1. 1

    Shut off power and water

    The breaker feeding the disposal is switched off and tested, and the sink supplies are closed if the trap will be removed.

  2. 2

    Remove the old unit

    The mounting ring is twisted free, the discharge tube and dishwasher hose are disconnected, and the unit is lowered out.

  3. 3

    Prepare the sink flange

    Old plumber's putty is cleaned off and the flange is reset with fresh putty or a gasket, with the mounting assembly tightened evenly.

  4. 4

    Hang the new unit

    The disposal locks onto the mounting ring. If a dishwasher is present, its inlet knockout is punched out before the hose goes on.

  5. 5

    Connect drain and power

    The discharge tube joins a trap with a proper slope, and the cord or wiring goes into a grounded, switched supply.

  6. 6

    Test for leaks

    Water runs for several minutes while the plumber checks the flange, dishwasher hose and trap for drips.

Questions that sort out the price on the phone

Having these answers ready makes the price discussion shorter and more accurate.

  • 1Is there already a disposal, or is this a first-time install?
  • 2Is there an outlet or switch under the sink, and is it a plug-in or a hardwired connection?
  • 3Does the dishwasher drain into the disposal, or through an air gap or high loop at the sink?
  • 4Is the sink a double bowl, a single bowl or a farmhouse style, since some old flanges do not fit new mounts?
  • 5Do you rely on town sewer or a septic system?
  • 6Is the existing trap plastic or metal, and is it leaking or slow?

Septic homes: think before you add a disposal

Disposals add ground food to the waste stream, which increases solids in a septic tank. Many manufacturers and health agencies advise septic owners to use a disposal sparingly or not at all, and a tank may need more frequent pumping if one is added.

Wareham's housing plan notes that areas north of Route 25 could not be added to the sewer system, so many homes in town rely on septic. Ask your septic inspector or the Board of Health before adding a disposal, and keep grease and coffee grounds out of the sink either way.

Sewer or septic in Wareham matters here

The Wareham Sewer Department operates about 61 miles of gravity sewer pipe and 45 pump stations, and the town has reported a major upgrade to its wastewater treatment facility. Properties on that system send food solids to a treatment plant, so a disposal is less of a burden than on a septic tank.

For homes on septic, Massachusetts requires a Title 5 inspection when the property is sold. An inspector looking at the tank sees whether solids are building up, which is one more reason to talk to the inspector before adding grinding load.

When the old disposal can be repaired instead

If the unit hums but will not spin, it may only be jammed, and a hex key in the bottom socket often frees it. A reset button on the base trips after overload. A leak at the top flange or dishwasher inlet is often a seal or clamp, and garbage disposal repair is the right route in those cases.

Replacement makes more sense when the housing is cracked, the motor shorts the breaker, or the unit leaks from the body seam. A body leak cannot be sealed, and a disposal that is over about 10 years old is usually not worth another repair.

Is a permit part of a disposal install?

Permit rules vary, so ask Wareham Inspectional Services before you start. 248 CMR 3.05 requires a permit before plumbing work is installed, altered or replaced, but the exemptions are narrow, which is one more reason to have a licensed plumber confirm the rule for your swap.
